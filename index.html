What the Zap did at each move

Press Run. Each DripJobs move shows up here with what the Zap did, and for every event it sends, the JSON and Meta's match check.

    Which DripJobs moves go to Meta

    In DripJobsZapier triggerMeta event name
    Lead lands in Cold Lead or Estimate RequestedNew Lead CreatedRaw Lead
    Estimate bookedDeal Stage Changed to Estimate ScheduledEstimate Scheduled
    Proposal goes outDeal Stage Changed to Proposal SentProposal Sent
    Customer accepts, job moves to the jobs pipelineProposal AcceptedJob Won
    Customer rejects, or your team marks it rejectedProposal RejectedLost

    Every other move (Warm Lead, and the job card's stages) is stopped by the filter. Meta's event name is free-form, so these can match your own stage wording, and a custom stage can get its own row. Meta wants the stage you optimize for to happen within 28 days of the lead.

    How each event is built and matched

    • Fields follow Meta's Conversion Leads payload spec: event_name is the stage, event_time is when the card moved (Unix seconds), action_source is system_generated, user_data.lead_id is the Facebook Lead ID, custom_data.event_source is crm and lead_event_source is DripJobs.
    • Meta matches on the Lead ID first, then click ID, then hashed email. It rejects an event with an invalid Lead ID, and may discard one timed before the lead was created or more than 7 days before sending.
    • DripJobs' Create Lead action has no custom-field input, so the Lead ID has to ride somewhere. In this demo the Zap looks it up on the GoHighLevel contact by email. It could also ride in the deal's Notes or name. The audit shows which way your current setup went.
    • On your accounts, the proof for each event is the DripJobs card, the Zap run and the event in your CRM dataset in Events Manager, side by side in screenshots, plus Diagnostics (no errors), and Lead Coverage (Meta wants 60% or higher) once Meta's 1 to 2 day data check is done.

    What this can't catch

    • A wrong stage picked by hand in DripJobs. If someone moves a card to the wrong stage, the event goes out looking correct, so no check can tell. Your team's stage moves are the source of truth.
    • Meta's own reports lag about 1 to 2 days. Meta runs a data check first, so its reports, like Lead Coverage, confirm a day or two after an event is sent.
    • Zapier only flags errors. A run that safely halts, a run a filter stopped and a trigger that never fires all look quiet in Zapier. That's why the Lead ID check, the nothing-found check and the weekly count exist.
